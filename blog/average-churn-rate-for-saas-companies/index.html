Average churn rate for SaaS companies: benchmarks by segment, and how to lower yours

Churn benchmarks for SaaS teams by price, customer size and B2B or B2C, how to measure your own rate properly, and eight lifecycle fixes that lower it.

There is no single average churn rate for SaaS companies, because churn depends on what you charge and who you sell to. Recurly reports a median churn rate of 3.22% for SaaS businesses on its billing network in July 2026. ChartMogul found median monthly customer churn of 6.1% for accounts paying under $25 a month, and 2.2% for accounts paying over $500 a month.

This guide gives churn benchmarks by segment, shows how to measure your own rate, and explains which lifecycle fixes lower it.

I lead brand, PR (public relations) and partnerships at Fourward, and Fourward also runs lifecycle marketing, so I have a stake in this topic. Every number below links to the publisher's own page, checked on 17 November 2026.

Key takeaways
  • A SaaS churn rate is the share of customers or recurring revenue you lose in a set period, usually a month or a year.
  • Price splits the benchmarks sharply. ChartMogul found 6.1% median monthly churn under $25 a month, and 2.2% over $500.
  • The 2025 KeyBanc and Sapphire Ventures SaaS survey puts gross retention near 90% and net retention above 100%.
  • Annual plans keep more revenue. At $250 to $500 a month per account, ChartMogul saw 88% net retention on annual plans and 76% on monthly plans.
  • Fix failed payments first. Recurly says a SaaS business above 5% annual churn should audit involuntary churn first.

What SaaS churn rate means

Churn is the customers or revenue you lose. Recurly defines churn rate as "the percentage of subscribers who cancel or fail to renew within a given period". SaaS teams track it in three pairs.

  • Customer churn and revenue churn. Customer churn counts the accounts that leave. Revenue churn counts the recurring revenue lost to cancellations and downgrades. One large account leaving hurts revenue churn far more than customer churn.
  • Gross and net retention. GRR (gross revenue retention) is the share of starting revenue you keep after cancellations and downgrades. NRR (net revenue retention) also adds upgrades from the same customers, so it can go above 100%.
  • Voluntary and involuntary churn. Voluntary churn is a customer choosing to cancel. Involuntary churn is a customer lost to a failed payment, such as an expired card.

Two more terms appear in the benchmarks. ARPA (average revenue per account) is what one customer pays you each month on average. ACV (annual contract value) is what one contract is worth per year.

The figures disagree because each study counts something different. ChartMogul's own metrics guide says it plainly: "There's no single 'average' churn rate".

  • Different units. Recurly counts subscribers who leave. The KeyBanc and Sapphire Ventures survey measures revenue kept.
  • Different periods. Recurly labels its figures annual. ChartMogul's price-band figures are monthly.
  • Different companies. Each study draws on its own customers or survey group, so the mix of prices and buyers differs.

Churn benchmarks by segment

Your segment matters more than any overall average. Compare your rate with companies that charge similar prices to similar buyers.

By price: cheaper products lose customers faster

Price splits the data sharply. In ChartMogul's numbers, median monthly customer churn is 6.1% when accounts pay under $25 a month. It falls to 2.2% when they pay over $500 a month.

SaaS Capital sees the same pattern by contract size. Its 2025 study says "higher net retention correlates with higher ACVs", and that companies with the highest ACVs have the highest gross retention.

By customer size: small business versus enterprise

Price is a rough stand-in for customer size, since enterprise contracts cost more than small-business plans. Recurly's data shows what changes as price rises.

  • Failed payments almost disappear. Involuntary churn is 0.18% for accounts paying $250 or more, against 1.25% across all industries.
  • Cancellations do not. Median churn for the $250 and over group is 3.54%, close to the 3.22% SaaS median.

So larger customers who leave mostly choose to leave. Keeping them depends on the product and the service, not on billing.

B2B versus B2C

Selling to businesses keeps more revenue than selling to consumers. In ChartMogul's December 2025 retention report, median NRR was 82% for B2B SaaS and 49% for B2C SaaS. The report sums it up: "B2C is much less sticky with minimal upsell." Recurly agrees that B2B businesses generally churn less than B2C ones.

ChartMogul's 82% sits well below the KeyBanc and Sapphire figure of above 100%. The two studies cover different companies, which is one more reason to benchmark inside your own segment.

AI-native products

The same ChartMogul report splits out AI-native products, meaning tools built around AI from the start. Those selling for under $50 a month keep 23% of revenue gross and 32% net. Those selling for over $250 a month keep 70% gross and 85% net. If you sell a low-priced AI tool, general SaaS benchmarks are not your comparison set.

How to calculate your SaaS churn rate

Recurly notes that churn "is typically calculated monthly or annually depending on your billing model". Pick one period, write down your counting rules, and use them every time. MRR (monthly recurring revenue) is the base for the revenue measures.

MetricFormulaWhat it tells you
Customer churn rateCustomers lost in the period, divided by customers at the start, times 100How many accounts leave
Gross revenue retentionStarting MRR minus cancelled and downgraded MRR, divided by starting MRR, times 100How much existing revenue you keep before upgrades
Net revenue retentionStarting MRR minus cancelled and downgraded MRR plus upgrades, divided by starting MRR, times 100Whether existing customers grow or shrink in total
Involuntary churn rateCustomers lost to failed payments, divided by customers at the start, times 100How much churn billing fixes could remove
  1. Count from the start of the period. Use customers at the start of the month as the base. For example, 400 customers at the start and 12 lost gives 12 divided by 400, or 3% monthly churn.
  2. Leave out same-period sign-ups. ChartMogul's formula counts "customers lost, minus those who both joined and churned". Track those quick exits separately, because they point to an onboarding or fit problem.
  3. Work out revenue retention too. Say you start the month with $100,000 in MRR. Cancellations take $5,000 and downgrades take $2,000, so GRR is 93%. Upgrades add $8,000, so NRR is 101%.
  4. Split voluntary from involuntary. Tag every lost account with a reason: cancelled by choice, or lost to a failed payment. The two need different fixes.
  5. Convert monthly to annual correctly. Annual churn is 1 minus (1 minus monthly churn) to the power of 12. At 3% a month you keep about 69% of customers, so annual churn is about 31%, not 36%.
  6. Cut the number by segment. Report churn by price band, plan type and sign-up month. One blended rate hides which customers are leaving.

What is a good churn rate for SaaS?

A good churn rate is one at or below the benchmark for your own price band and buyer type. These reference points come from the sources above.

  • Revenue. GRR near 90% and NRR above 100% match the 2025 KeyBanc and Sapphire Ventures survey.
  • Customers, higher price. Monthly customer churn near 2.2% matches ChartMogul's median for accounts paying over $500 a month.
  • Customers, lower price. Monthly customer churn near 6.1% matches ChartMogul's median under $25 a month. That is the typical rate, not a goal.
  • Warning sign. Recurly says "If your SaaS business is running above 5% annual churn, involuntary churn is worth auditing first".

Read GRR and NRR together. Upgrades can push NRR above 100% while more customers leave each quarter. GRR shows that loss, because upgrades cannot cover it.

How to lower churn across the customer lifecycle

Churn has causes at every stage, from the first sales call to the renewal date. Start with failed payments, as Recurly suggests, then work back through the lifecycle.

1. Recover failed payments

Failed payments are often the simplest churn to recover, because the customer never chose to leave. Stripe's Smart Retries "chooses the best times to retry failed payment attempts". New Stripe accounts get up to 8 retries within 2 weeks by default.

Retries cannot fix every failure. After a hard decline, where the card's bank refuses the charge outright, Stripe only collects once the customer adds a new payment method. So send an email with a card-update link as soon as a payment fails.

2. Move customers to annual plans

ChartMogul's 2025 billing report found a clear gap between billing terms.

  • At $250 to $500 per account a month, median NRR is 88% on annual plans and 76% on monthly plans.
  • Under $25 per account a month, median customer retention is 62% on annual plans and 41% on monthly plans.

Some of that gap may come from who picks annual plans, not from the billing term itself. Still, offering annual billing at sign-up and at each renewal is a low-cost test.

3. Sell to customers who fit

Some churn starts before the contract is signed. A customer sold the wrong promise leaves when the product does not match it. My work covers positioning, and a clear statement of who the product is for is the first filter. Our guide to B2B brand building covers how to set that position. Then review lost accounts against the original deal notes, and share the patterns with sales.

4. Get new customers to first value fast

Customers who join and leave in the same month never reached what they paid for. Name the first action that shows value, such as a first report sent or a first teammate invited. Then build onboarding emails and in-app prompts that lead to that action, triggered by what the customer does rather than by the calendar.

5. Watch usage and step in early

Watch for signals that an account may be at risk. Set an alert for each signal below and give it a named owner.

  • Fewer log-ins from the main users over several weeks.
  • Unused seats the customer is paying for.
  • A champion who leaves, meaning the person who bought your product changes job.
  • Support tickets that repeat the same complaint.

6. Run renewals as a process

An annual renewal is a decision the customer makes on a date you already know. Start the conversation months before that date. Review what the customer achieved, what they still need, and who signs the renewal.

7. Ask why customers leave

Add a one-question cancellation survey with fixed reasons, such as price, a missing feature, a switch to another tool, or no longer needed. Read the answers every month and send the patterns to product and sales.

8. Turn retained customers into proof

Customers who stay and get results can help you win the next customer. My work covers reviews and listings, and retained customers are the people I would ask first. Ask them for reviews on the sites where buyers compare SaaS tools, and for a case study once their results are clear. Honest reviews also set clearer expectations for the next buyer.

Common churn measurement mistakes

  • Comparing a monthly rate with an annual benchmark. Check the period on every benchmark, including the ones in this guide.
  • Multiplying monthly churn by 12. Compounding makes the real annual figure lower: 3% a month is about 31% a year, not 36%.
  • Mixing customer churn and revenue churn. Losing many small accounts can raise customer churn while revenue churn barely moves.
  • Letting NRR hide losses. Report GRR next to NRR, so upgrades from some customers do not mask cancellations from others.
  • Treating failed payments as cancellations. Failed payments need billing fixes, not product fixes.
  • Using one blended rate. Split churn by price band and sign-up month before you decide what to fix.

FAQs

What is the average churn rate for SaaS companies?

Recurly reports a median churn rate of 3.22% for SaaS businesses on its network in July 2026, which it labels annual. ChartMogul's monthly medians run from 6.1% for accounts under $25 a month to 2.2% for accounts over $500. Compare your rate within your own price band.

What is a good monthly churn rate for SaaS?

It depends on price. ChartMogul's median monthly customer churn is 2.2% for accounts paying over $500 a month and 6.1% for accounts paying under $25. Beating the median for your band is a fair first target.

How do you calculate SaaS churn rate?

Divide the customers lost during the period by the customers you had at the start, then multiply by 100. Leave out customers who joined and left in the same period, and track revenue churn alongside it.

What is the difference between GRR and NRR?

GRR (gross revenue retention) counts revenue kept after cancellations and downgrades, so it cannot go above 100%. NRR (net revenue retention) also adds upgrades from existing customers. The 2025 KeyBanc and Sapphire Ventures survey puts GRR near 90% and NRR above 100%.

What is involuntary churn?

Involuntary churn is customers lost to failed payments rather than a choice to cancel. Recurly reports median involuntary churn of 1.25% across all industries, and 0.18% for accounts paying $250 or more.
