How to choose a B2B SaaS marketing agency: a 7-step guide

Pick the type of agency before the name, match it to your stage, and check the things you can verify on their site and in one call. Here is the process, step by step, with a two-week plan.

To choose a B2B SaaS marketing agency, decide the type of agency you need before you look at names, match it to your stage, check for named SaaS results on its own site, make sure the price and minimum term fit, talk to a client at your size, find out who will actually do the work, and agree on pipeline reporting before you sign. Done in that order, the shortlist mostly picks itself.

I lead search at Fourward and I've sat on both sides of this call. This guide is the process I'd use. When you're ready for names, our list of the best B2B SaaS marketing agencies compares 14 on the same questions.

1. Name the one problem you're hiring for

"We need marketing" produces vague proposals. "We need 30 qualified demos a month from paid search by Q2" produces a quote you can judge. Write down the one result you'd be happy with in six months, in numbers. Pipeline, demos, sign-ups or revenue. Not traffic, not followers.

Then write down what you already have: a person, a channel that works, a CRM, content. The agency should add to that, not replace it.

2. Match the agency to your stage

Agencies that know who they're for say so. Kalungi says it works best with SaaS companies past product-market fit, around $5M to $50M ARR. Refine Labs focuses on Series B and later. Hey Digital targets Series A and B. Powered by Search is one of the few that lists on its site who it isn't for.

Key takeaway

If an agency's site doesn't say what stage it serves, ask on the first call. If the answer is "everyone", that's an answer too.

3. Look for named SaaS results on their own site

Every agency has a results page. Look for three things: a named client, a number tied to pipeline or revenue rather than traffic, and a timeframe. "Grew organic traffic 300%" with no client name is hard to check. "16x increase in opportunities for TrueFort" is a claim you can follow up.

Also check the clients are SaaS. A B2B agency with great results for a manufacturer may not understand trials, demos, churn or product-led sign-ups.

4. Check the price and term before the call

Ten of the 14 agencies in our guide publish at least one price. Reading those first saves calls you can't afford. A focused program from a smaller agency runs $4,000 to $10,000 a month, a specialist or full team $14,000 to $50,000 and more, with ad spend on top. I listed every published figure in SaaS marketing agency pricing.

Minimum terms of three to twelve months are normal. Decide what term you can commit to before someone persuades you.

5. Talk to a client at your size

Logo walls show who paid once. A reference call shows what working together is like. Ask for a current client at roughly your ARR and in a similar sales motion, and ask them three things: who did the work day to day, how long until results showed, and what they'd change.

An agency that can't produce one such client is telling you something about its fit, not its honesty.

6. Find out who does the work

The people on the pitch call are often not the people on your account. Ask for the names of the strategist, the specialists and the account lead, and how many other accounts each of them runs. Bay Leaf, for example, puts a growth marketing manager and a senior strategist on every account and says so on its site. That's the kind of specific answer you want from everyone.

Also ask how much is done by people and how much by software or AI. Neither is wrong, but you should know what you're paying for.

7. Agree on what gets reported

Before you sign, agree on the three or four numbers you'll see every month and where they come from. For B2B SaaS that's usually qualified pipeline, demos or trials, cost per qualified lead and, over time, revenue. Clicks, impressions and rankings are inputs, not results.

Put it in the contract. It's the single best protection against a relationship that drifts into busywork.

A note on AI search

Most agencies now sell AI search work, called GEO or AEO. Eleven of the 14 in our guide do. Ask what it actually includes: crawler access, content for buyer questions, structured data, mentions on the sites AI cites, and how they measure it. Red flags

  • No clear "not a fit". Good agencies turn work away.
  • Results without client names. Anonymous percentages can't be checked.
  • A senior pitch team you never see again. Ask who runs the account after kickoff.
  • Reports built on traffic. Ask how the work connects to demos and revenue.
  • A long lock-in with no exit. A minimum term is normal. No way out if results don't come is not.
  • Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed pipeline. Nobody controls Google or your sales team.

A two-week selection process

  1. Days 1–2: write the brief. One result, one budget range, one term you can commit to, what you already have.
  2. Days 3–5: shortlist three. Pick the type first, then three agencies of that type whose stage and published pricing fit. Start from the best B2B SaaS marketing agencies.
  3. Days 6–10: first calls. Thirty minutes each. Use the questions to ask a marketing agency. Ask for a reference and a written proposal with a price.
  4. Days 11–13: references and proposals. Call the references. Compare proposals on scope, people and reporting, not on page count.
  5. Day 14: decide. If no one fits, that's a result too. Widen the type or the budget, not your standards.

FAQs

How do I choose a B2B SaaS marketing agency?

Decide the type of agency you need first, then match it to your stage, check for named SaaS results on its site, confirm the price and minimum term fit, talk to a client at your size, find out who does the work, and agree on pipeline reporting before you sign.

What should I look for in a SaaS marketing agency?

SaaS named as a core market, clients at your stage, results stated in pipeline or revenue with the client named, a clear price or a fast path to one, and named people on your account.

How much should I budget for a B2B SaaS marketing agency?

From the published prices in our guide, $4,000 to $10,000 a month for a focused program, $14,000 to $30,000 for a specialist or two channels, and $50,000 or more for a full outsourced team, before ad spend.

Should a SaaS startup hire an agency or build in-house?

Before product-market fit, a specialist on one channel is usually enough. After it, the choice depends on how many channels you need at once and what you can spend. A four-person in-house team costs about $564,824 in year one at US medians.

How long does it take to choose an agency?

Two weeks is enough if you write the brief first: a shortlist of three, a 30-minute call with each, references and a written proposal, then a decision.

What questions should I ask a marketing agency?

Who will work on the account, what the minimum term is, whether you can talk to a client at your stage, what gets reported, and what they need from you. We list 25 in a separate post.
