What AI crawler software does

An AI crawler is a bot that an AI company sends to read web pages, for model training, for AI search or to fetch a page a user asked about. AI crawler software watches those bots and acts on them. AI crawler software does up to three jobs.

  • See. The software records which AI bots asked for which pages, and how often. Cloudflare says its AI Crawl Control "gives you visibility into which AI services are accessing your content".
  • Control. The software allows or blocks each bot, by writing robots.txt rules or by rejecting requests at the firewall. Robots.txt is a plain text file on your site that asks bots to stay out of certain pages.
  • Charge. A few tools let you set a price for AI access instead of a plain yes or no. Paid access is still early, and it mainly suits publishers.

Robots.txt alone cannot do the first job, because the file never shows who visited. Robots.txt also cannot stop a bot that ignores it, or a spoofed bot, which pretends to be a different, trusted bot. Known Agents, for example, says it can "Stop unwanted bots from accessing your content, even when they ignore robots.txt or spoof trusted identities".

The four kinds of AI crawler software

The tools fall into four groups. Where a tool sits decides what it can see and what it can stop.

  • CDN and hosting bot controls. A CDN is a service that sits between your website and its visitors, so every request passes through it. A CDN can see each AI bot and block it before the bot reaches your site. Cloudflare and Vercel both offer AI bot controls.
  • Agent analytics tools. An agent analytics tool is a dedicated dashboard for visits from bots and AI agents. Some agent analytics tools also generate your robots.txt for you. Known Agents is one example.
  • AI access and payment platforms. An AI access platform lets you charge AI companies for reading your pages. TollBit and Cloudflare's pay per crawl work this way.
  • Log analysers and access checkers. A log file analyser reads your server logs, the record your server keeps of every request. An access checker tests whether named AI bots can get in. Neither kind blocks anything. Screaming Frog sells a log analyser of this kind.

Cloudflare AI Crawl Control

AI Crawl Control is Cloudflare's dashboard for AI bots, and Cloudflare's documentation says it was formerly called AI Audit. The same page says it "Works automatically on all Cloudflare plans". Cloudflare tells site owners to "Monitor the dashboard to see crawler activity and request patterns".

Cloudflare's AI crawler access settings let you "set actions for Search, Agent, and Training traffic". Separate actions let a SaaS site keep AI search bots in while it blocks training bots.

Vercel AI bots managed ruleset

Vercel is a hosting platform for websites, and its firewall includes a ruleset for AI bots. Vercel's documentation says the ruleset can "log or deny these requests" from known AI crawlers. The same page says the "AI bots managed ruleset is available on all plans".

Vercel says it adds new AI bots to its list automatically and applies the action you already chose. The Vercel ruleset is off by default, so nothing changes until you switch it on.

Known Agents

Known Agents is a dedicated agent analytics tool, and the darkvisitors.com pricing address now redirects to it. The Known Agents homepage promises: "See every crawler, scraper, and AI agent visiting your website in real time". Known Agents can also generate a robots.txt that "updates automatically as new bots are discovered".

  • Free: $0 a month for 1 project and 100K events.
  • Startup: $29 a month for 3 projects and 1M events.
  • Growth: $299 a month for 10 projects and 10M events.
  • Enterprise: custom pricing, according to the Known Agents pricing page.

TollBit

TollBit is built for sites that want to charge AI companies for access. TollBit says it "provides analytics for AI traffic", and its homepage lists "Usage-based pricing for AI access, on your terms". TollBit also says it "gives publishers a free platform for understanding how agents interact with their sites".

A SaaS marketing site usually wants AI tools to read its pages for free, so charging rarely fits. Cloudflare offers a similar option called pay per crawl, which its documentation marks as a private beta.

Screaming Frog Log File Analyser

The Log File Analyser reads your server log files and costs £99 a year. The free version is limited to 1,000 log lines and 1 project. Screaming Frog lists the feature "Automatically verify search bots such as Googlebot, and view IPs spoofing requests".

Server logs record every request, including bots that ignored robots.txt. Our guide to Screaming Frog alternatives covers other tools that read server logs.

How to choose AI crawler software for a SaaS site

Start from where your site runs and what you need to know. These five rules narrow the six options down.

  • Your site runs on Cloudflare. Start with AI Crawl Control, because Cloudflare already sees every request. Cloudflare says "For new domains, Search, Agents, and Training use Allow (do not block) by default", so check your own settings.
  • Your site runs on Vercel. Turn on the AI bots ruleset in log mode first. Log mode records AI bot requests, so you see the traffic before you deny anything.
  • You run several sites or use another host. An agent analytics tool such as Known Agents is a separate product from your host. Known Agents' paid plans include 3 or 10 projects, and the free plan includes 1.
  • You need proof of what happened. Read your server logs with a log file analyser. Logs record every request, including requests from bots that ignored your rules.
  • You want to be paid for access. Look at TollBit or Cloudflare's pay per crawl. Most SaaS marketing sites want AI tools to read and cite them, so paid access rarely applies.

Four questions to ask any vendor

  • Does it separate search bots from training bots? A single switch for every AI bot cannot keep search bots in while it blocks training bots.
  • Does it check that a bot is real? A bot can send a false name, so look for verification, not only name matching.
  • Does its bot list update itself? AI companies add new bots over time, and Vercel, for example, adds new AI bots to its list automatically.
  • Can it block, or only report? Reports show which bots visit, but blocking needs a firewall, a CDN or a supported integration.

How to set up AI crawler software in five steps

  1. Check what bots can reach today. Fourward's free AI crawler checker tests 20 AI crawlers against your robots.txt and your firewall. Note any bot that is blocked by accident.
  2. Write down your policy. Decide which bots to allow, usually AI search bots and user fetchers, the bots that open a page when a person asks an AI tool to read it. Our guide to what an AI crawler is lists the main bot names and their jobs.
  3. Pick one tool for each job you need. Use your CDN or host to block, an analytics tool to report, and a log analyser to audit.
  4. Watch before you block. Run the tool in log or report-only mode first. Block a bot only once you know what it does and whether it helps buyers find you.
  5. Make robots.txt and the firewall agree. A bot you allow in robots.txt still gets turned away if the firewall blocks it. Review both settings every quarter.

Mistakes to avoid with AI crawler software

  • Blocking every AI bot with one switch. A block on AI search bots can keep your pages out of AI answers. Our guide on how to get cited by ChatGPT explains OpenAI's rule for its search bot.
  • Assuming the default suits you. Cloudflare's stated default of allowing AI bots applies to new domains, so an older account may be set differently. Vercel's AI bots ruleset stays off until you turn it on.
  • Trusting the name a bot sends. A spoofed bot uses a trusted bot's name. Choose a tool that verifies bots, as Screaming Frog's Log File Analyser does for search bots.
  • Mixing up crawler software and AI visibility tools. Crawler software shows which bots read your site. An AI visibility tool shows what AI answers say about you, which is a different question.
  • Paying for reports nobody reads. A free plan is enough until one person owns the data and acts on it.

FAQs

What is AI crawler software?

AI crawler software is a tool that shows which AI bots visit your website and lets you allow, block or charge them. Examples include Cloudflare AI Crawl Control, Vercel's AI bots ruleset, Known Agents and TollBit.

Is there free software to see which AI bots crawl my site?

Yes. Cloudflare says AI Crawl Control works on all Cloudflare plans, and Known Agents has a free plan with 1 project and 100K events. Screaming Frog's free Log File Analyser reads up to 1,000 log lines.

Does Cloudflare block AI crawlers by default?

No, not for new domains. Cloudflare's documentation, updated 2 November 2026, says new domains allow Search, Agent and Training bots by default. You can set a different action for each of the three.

Can I charge AI crawlers to read my site?

Yes, with a few tools. TollBit offers usage-based pricing for AI access, and Cloudflare's pay per crawl is in private beta. Most SaaS marketing sites want AI tools to read them, so charging rarely fits.

Do I need AI crawler software if I already have robots.txt?

Robots.txt asks bots to stay out, but it cannot show who visited or stop a bot that ignores it. AI crawler software adds the record of visits and, through a firewall or CDN, the block.
